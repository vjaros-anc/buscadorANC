         (‘International Paper’, ‘IP’ or the ‘Notifying Party’, United States) acquired, by
      way of purchase of shares, sole control over DS Smith Plc (‘DS Smith’ or ‘DSS’,

      DSS are hereinafter jointly designated as the ‘Parties’.
(2)      In particular, under the Commitments IP undertakes to divest to an independent
       and suitable purchaser:
          (a)   Three IP plants located in Normandy, France, namely: a box plant in Saint-
           Amand, a box plant in Mortagne, and a sheet plant in Cabourg (owned by

               respectively), all of which manufacture and supply conventional corrugated

             Vizcaya, S.L.U.).

          jointly, as ‘Divestment Businesses’.
(3)     The Divestment Businesses include, in particular, all assets and staff, to the extent
        they contribute to the current operation or are necessary to ensure the viability and
        competitiveness of the Divestment Businesses, in particular:
          (a)    all tangible and intangible assets (including products under development
            and related rights, intellectual property rights, and know-how);
         (b)    all  licences, permits and  authorisations  issued by any governmental
              organisation for the benefit of the Divestment Businesses;
          (c)    all contracts, leases, commitments, and customer orders of the Divestment
              Businesses; all customers, credits, and other records of the Divestment
              Businesses; all cooperation agreements with third parties; and
         (d)    all staff currently employed by the Divestment Businesses, including staff
             seconded to the Divestment Businesses, shared personnel as well as the
               additional personnel listed in the Schedule of the Commitments.
(4)     The Commitments also include the benefit, for a transitional period, on terms and
        conditions equivalent to those at present afforded to the Divestment Businesses,
        of the current arrangements under which IP or its affiliated undertakings supply
        products or services to the Divestment Businesses or of any other current or new
        arrangements which are necessary for the viability of the Divestment Businesses,
        unless otherwise agreed with the purchaser and with the approval of the
       Commission. These include the supply of corrugated case materials (‘CCM’),
         centrally procured sourcing (such as energy, insurance policies, starch, and ink),
       and services (such as IT systems, payroll and finance services) by IP to the
        Divestment Businesses, under the terms described in the Commitments.
(5)      In addition, the Notifying Party undertakes, subject to customary limitations, not
         to solicit the key personnel transferred with the Divestment Businesses and
        necessary to maintain the  viability and competitiveness of the Divestment

2.    ASSESSMENT OF THE PROPOSAL

2.1.    Legal framework
(11)    According to paragraph 16 of the Commitments, the Commission has to verify
         that the Divestment Businesses are being sold in a manner consistent with the
       Commitments and that the purchaser fulfils the purchaser criteria. According to
        paragraph 15 of the Commitments, in its assessment of the proposed purchaser,
         the Commission should ensure that:
          (a)  The purchaser is independent of and unconnected to the Notifying Party and
                   its affiliated undertakings.
         (b)  The purchaser has the financial resources, proven expertise, and incentive
               to maintain and develop the Divestment Businesses as viable and active
              competitive forces in competition with the Parties and other competitors.


(2)       Specifically, the Proposed Agreement is concluded on behalf of Palm by the wholly owned
         subsidiaries Palm Industrie Beteiligungs-GmbH (Germany) and Seyfert SAS (France).

        performance, with a healthy implied EBITDA profitability margin in 2024 that,
        moreover, has been stable over time. (13)
(25)     Third, Palm’s financial position and leverage appear to be sustainable. (14) This is
         consistent with the financial analysis carried out by the Trustee, which finds that

(29)     In view of the information provided by the Notifying Party and the Trustee, the

         the Divestment Businesses as viable competitive forces.
(30)     First, Palm is an established operator with sufficient experience in the corrugated
        packaging  industry. Palm was founded  in 1872 and  is specialized in the

        packaging. It currently has around 4 200 employees across its three business
         divisions. (18)
(31)    Second, Palm has an international presence, operating in 34 manufacturing
         locations (including five paper mills and 29 corrugated packaging plants) and two
         recycling companies, across Germany, France, Italy, the Netherlands, the United
       Kingdom, and Switzerland. (19)
(32)     Third, as described in Section 2.3.2.1 above, its track record suggests that Palm
        has the required expertise to integrate the activities of the Divestment Businesses.
       Palm first entered the French market with dedicated local operations by acquiring
         the Seyfert group in 2014. The data submitted by Palm shows that it managed to
         successfully integrate the activities of this company. In particular, it appears that
       Palm would have the necessary expertise to replicate this success in entering the
        Portuguese and Spanish markets by acquiring the Divestment Businesses. (20)
(33)   On the basis of the information provided by the Parties and taking into account
         the Reasoned Opinion, the Commission therefore concludes that Palm has

         competitors.

2.3.4.   Absence of prima facie competition problems
(44)    Under paragraph 15(iv) of the Commitments, the transfer of the Divestment
        Businesses to a suitable purchaser must neither be likely to create prima facie
        competition concerns nor give rise to a risk that the implementation of the
       Commitments will be delayed.
(45)    The acquisition by Palm of the Divestment Businesses does not give rise to prima
         facie competition problems. The acquisition of the Divestment Businesses does
        not create horizontal overlaps between the activities of the Divestment Businesses
       and Palm in Portugal or in Spain because Palm does not currently operate in these
         countries. (28)
(46)    Limited  horizontal overlaps  arise  in  the  local markets  in France  for  the
        manufacture and supply of corrugated cases. However, as mentioned by the
        Trustee in its Reasoned Opinion, the combined market shares in the relevant local
        markets would be less than 10%. (29) In addition, Palm’s acquisition of the
        Divestment Businesses gives rise to limited vertical relationships. In both the
        upstream and downstream relevant markets, however, the combined market
         shares would remain well below 30%. (30)
(47)    This prima facie assessment is based on the information available for purposes of
          this purchaser approval. The acquisition of the Divestment Businesses by Palm is
         subject to notification to the French national competition authority, pursuant to
         the applicable French merger control rules. On 20 June 2025, the French national
        competition authority unconditionally approved the transaction, following a
         simplified procedure investigation that is applicable to unproblematic merger
         cases.
(48)     In view of the above, the Commission considers that Palm’s acquisition of the
        Divestment Businesses does not give rise to prima facie competition issues and
         that the transaction is unlikely to lead to implementation delays.

(49)     In light of the above considerations and taking into account the Reasoned Opinion
