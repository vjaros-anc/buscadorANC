Commission approves Kimberly-Clark's acquisition of Kenvue, subject to
conditions

Brussels, 13 October 2026

The European Commission has approved, under the EU Merger Regulation, the proposed acquisition
by Kimberly-Clark Corporation ('Kimberly-Clark') of Kenvue Inc ('Kenvue'). The approval is
conditional upon full compliance with the commitments offered by Kimberly-Clark.

Kimberly-Clark is globally active in the production and sale of personal care products for personal,
business, and industrial use. Its brand portfolio includes Huggies and Kotex, among others.

Kenvue is active in the manufacturing and supply of consumer health products. Its brands include
Listerine, Nicorette, Neutrogena, and o.b, among others.

The Commission's investigation

The Commission had concerns that the transaction, as initially notified, would reduce competition in
the markets for the supply of tampons in particular in Czechia, Estonia, and Slovakia. The
Commission's investigation found that the transaction would result in the removal of an important
competitor in these markets, where already few other suppliers are present.

The proposed remedies

To address the Commission's preliminary competition concerns, Kimberly-Clark offered to divest all
its tampon and other feminine hygiene brands in the European Economic Area ('EEA'), including
intellectual property rights, know-how, and associated key employees and relationships. The brands
covered by the divestment are Kotex (including the Lidie sub-brand), Camelia, and Record.

These commitments fully address the competition concerns identified by the Commission by
removing the horizontal overlap between the companies' activities in the EEA.

Following the feedback received in the context of the market test and consequent amendments to the
commitments, the Commission concluded that the transaction, as modified by the commitments,
would no longer raise competition concerns.

In today's decision, the Commission has also approved Dilek Gıda Üretim ve Pazarlama Ticaret A.Ş.
('Dilek') as the purchaser of the divested business. Following a careful review of Dilek's business
plan with the help of an independent adviser, the Commission concluded that Dilek is a suitable
purchaser given that it has, among others, the financial resources to maintain and develop the
divested business, as well as the expertise to operate it effectively as a viable and active competitive
force.

The decision is conditional upon full compliance with the commitments. Under the supervision of the
Commission, an independent trustee will monitor their implementation.

Companies and products

Kimberly-Clark, headquartered in the US, is globally active in the production and sale of personal
care products for personal, business, and industrial use. In the EEA, Kimberly-Clark supplies diapers
for infants and children, adult incontinence products, and feminine hygiene products. Kimberly-Clark's
brand portfolio includes, among others, Huggies and Kotex.

Kenvue, headquartered in the US, is active in the manufacturing and supply of consumer health
products. In the EEA, Kenvue supplies over-the-counter drugs, skin health and beauty products, baby
care products, feminine hygiene products, and essential health products such as mouthwash and
toothpaste. Kenvue's brands include, among others, Listerine, Nicorette, Neutrogena, and o.b.

Dilek, headquartered in Turkey, is a distributor of leading brands in the fast-moving consumer goods
sector.

Merger control rules and procedure

The transaction was notified to the Commission on 25 August 2026.

The Commission has the duty to assess mergers and acquisitions involving companies with a turnover
above certain thresholds (see Article 1 of the EU Merger Regulation) and to prevent concentrations
that would significantly impede effective competition in the EEA or any substantial part of it.

The vast majority of notified mergers do not pose competition problems and are cleared after a
routine review. From the moment a transaction is notified, the Commission generally has 25 working
days to decide whether to grant approval (Phase I) or to start an in-depth investigation (Phase II). If
commitments are proposed in Phase I, the Commission has 10 additional working days, bringing the
total duration of a Phase I case to 35 working days, such as in this case.

For more information

More information will be available on the Commission's competition website, in the public case
register under the case number M.12330.
