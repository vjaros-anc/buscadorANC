        Case M.10806 – BROADCOM / VMWARE


1.  INTRODUCTION

1.  On 15 November 2022, the Commission received a notification of a proposed
     concentration with Union dimension pursuant to Article 4 of the Merger Regulation2
    by which Broadcom, Inc.3 (‘Broadcom’ or ‘Notifying Party’) intends to acquire,
     within the meaning of Article 3(1)(b) of the Merger Regulation, sole control over
    VMware, Inc.4 (‘VMware’ and together with Broadcom the ‘Parties’) (the ‘Proposed
     Transaction’).

2.  PROCEDURE

     2.1.    Article 6(1) decision

2.  On 20 December 2022, the Commission adopted a decision to initiate proceedings
     pursuant to Article 6(1)(c) of the Merger Regulation (the ‘Article 6(1)(c) Decision’),
     as the Phase I investigation raised serious doubts as to the compatibility of the
    Proposed Transaction with the internal market. It further provided key documents to
     the Notifying Party on 21 December 2022.

3.  On 13 January 2023, the Notifying Party submitted its response to the Article 6(1)(c)
     Decision.

     3.1.   Access to file following the Oral Hearing

16.  Following the Oral Hearing, on the evening of 8 May 2023, Broadcom’s legal counsel
     sent an additional and supplementary Article 7 Request (the ‘Second Article 7
     Request’). In the Second Article 7 Request Broadcom asked to have further access to:
      (i) the non-confidential version of the Data Room Report claiming that the non-
     confidential version of the Report had been excessively redacted; (ii) a number of
     replies to one of the market test questionnaires9 sent to third parties in the context of

     principle, include recordings and/or verbatim transcripts of these calls.

17.  In light of the above, I considered that the substance of the First Article 7 Request had
    been partially fulfilled by DG Competition (see paragraph 10 above) and partially
     replaced by the Second Article 7 Request and I informed Broadcom accordingly on
    25 May 2023.

18.  Concerning the Second Article 7 Request, on 23 June I adopted a decision under
     Article 7 of the Hearing Officer Terms of Reference rejecting the Second Article 7
     Request. I informed Broadcom accordingly on 24 June 2023.

19. As outlined at paragraph 16 above, in the Second Article 7 Request, Broadcom
     stressed that the Commission’s file did not contain ‘verbatim transcripts’ and/or
     recordings of the Commission’s calls with the market participants, but exclusively
    agreed minutes of the calls. Broadcom claimed that these agreed minutes do not
     constitute accurate records of the third party’s actual statements, and as regards a
    number of calls, requested access to copies of all drafts of the minutes exchanged
    between the Commission and third parties.

20. The Court of Justice of the European Union (‘CJEU’) has interpreted Article 19(1) of
     Regulation 1/200310 and Article 3 of Regulation 773/200411 in a series of judgments12
     clarifying the scope of the Commission’s duty to record statements made during
     interviews intended to gather information for the purposes of an investigation.
    Broadcom’s request went beyond the requirements placed on the Commission by this
     case law, which does not apply directly to proceedings conducted under the Merger
     Regulation.13

21.  There is no basis, in my view, to require ‘verbatim transcripts’ or integral audio
     recordings of interviews or telephone calls in merger proceedings. I see no reason
   why more stringent recording requirements would apply in merger cases than in
     proceedings conducted under Regulation 1/2003. As long as the Commission
     provides access, upon request by the addressee of the SO, to a reliable and sufficiently
     detailed record of the contents of a meeting or call intended to gather information
     relevant to the investigation (such as agreed minutes of the conversation), the rights
     of defence are, in principle, sufficiently safeguarded.

22.  I rejected the request to access all the exchanges concerning the preparation of agreed
     minutes, taking into account, among other reasons: the manifestly confidential nature
     of the exchanges (which mainly concerned confidentiality claims by third parties
    under Article 18 of Regulation 802/2004); the absence of any indicia suggesting that
     the agreed minutes were somehow misleading or inaccurate; and the fact that
    Broadcom had access, for most calls, not only to the agreed minutes but also to the
    agenda shared by DG Competition with the third parties prior to each call.

     3.2.   Third extension of the time limit

23. On 12 May 2023, the Phase II investigation period was extended by 3 working days
      at the request of and in agreement with the Notifying Party pursuant to the first
     sentence of the second subparagraph of Article 10(3) of the Merger Regulation.

4.  COMMITMENTS OFFER AND MARKET TEST

24. On 16 May 2023, the Notifying Party submitted commitments, with a view to
     rendering the Proposed Transaction compatible with the internal market and the
     functioning of the EEA Agreement, pursuant to Article 8(2) of the Merger Regulation
     (the ‘Initial Commitments’).

25. On 23 May 2023, the Commission launched a market test of the Initial Commitments
     submitted by the Notifying Party.

26. The Commission gave the Notifying Party detailed feedback on the outcome of the
    market test on 8 June 2023.

27. On 14  June  2023,  following  the Commission’s  observations on  the  Initial
    Commitments, the Notifying Party submitted a modified set of commitments pursuant
     to Article 8(2) of the Merger Regulation in order to better address the competition
    concerns identified in the SO (the ‘Final Commitments’).

      4.1.   Access to file following the Final Commitments

28. On 29 June 2023,  the day before  the Advisory Committee took  place, DG
    Competition granted access to three additional documents to the Parties by means of
    an encrypted ‘.zip’ folder sent by email.

5.  THE DRAFT DECISION

29. The draft decision declares the Proposed Transaction compatible with the internal
    market and  the EEA Agreement,  subject  to  full compliance with  the  Final
    Commitments.

30.  Pursuant to Article 16(1) of Decision 2011/695, the Final Report shall consider
    whether the draft decision deals only with objections in respect of which the parties
    have been afforded the opportunity of making known their views.

31. Upon review of the draft decision, I conclude that it does not deal with any objection
     in respect of which the Parties have not been afforded the opportunity of making
    known their views.

6.  CONCLUSION

32.  I conclude that the effective exercise of the procedural rights of the Parties has been
     respected in this case.
