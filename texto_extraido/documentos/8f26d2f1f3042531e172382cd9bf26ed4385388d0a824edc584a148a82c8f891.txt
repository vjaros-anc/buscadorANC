(28)    In the first place, as indicated above, easyJet is a long-standing airline operating
        within the EEA. It has extensive experience in providing passenger air transport
         services within the EEA, including at primary airports. Operating flights on the
        Short-Haul Remedy Routes and the Short-Haul Direct / Indirect Remedy Routes
         represents an opportunity for easyJet with regard to its brand position in Italy as a
        whole, to expand its presence in LIN and to rapidly establish scale in FCO.24

(29)    In the second place, Italy, is already an important market of easyJet, where easyJet
           is committed to grow and where it has made significant investments in the recent
         years.25

(30)    In the third place, easyJet entered into the agreements as a single remedy taker for
           all Short-Haul Remedy Routes and Short-Haul Direct / Indirect Remedy Routes
       and for the slots at LIN. Being a single remedy-taker, easyJet is more likely to
        reach a scale of operations sufficient to allow  it to operate on the Short-Haul
      Remedy Routes during the minimum period and beyond, on the long-term. In
          particular, easyJet will have the opportunity to base a number of aircraft at FCO
       and at LIN.26 The investment required to open these bases indicates easyJet’s
          interest in the commercial opportunities its market entry presents.27

(31)    Second, while easyJet has previously submitted to the Commission that “airports
         in Rome are not a focus for easyJet”,28 it has submitted to the Monitoring Trustee
         that the larger number of FCO Short-Haul Remedy Routes and Short-Haul Direct /
         Indirect Remedy Routes included in the Commitments render such a base feasible
       from a fleet size and profitability perspective.29

(32)     Third, data on profitability from other routes in easyJet’s portfolio illustrates that
         easyJet  is  likely  to operate the Short-Haul Remedy Routes  at the projected
          profitability. The Commission has specifically considered the Contribution Per
       Block Hour (‘CPBH’) data provided by easyJet to the Monitoring Trustee for the
        purpose of assessing the strength of easyJet’s business case.30 Based on the CPBH
        data and a number of examples provided by easyJet, the Monitoring Trustee
        determined a benchmark CPBH to be used as a proxy to assess whether easyJet
       would decide to cancel a route (“Route Cancellation CPBH”) or a base (“Base
        Cancellation CPBH”). A possible incentive to cancel is identified in each case
       where the CPBH for a certain route or base is below the relevant benchmark. The
       Commission considers that these benchmarks were appropriately chosen for the

(130)   The Commission finds that the transfer of the additional LIN slots from the Parties
         to IAG and AFKLM pursuant to the terms described in paragraph (3) will further
         increase competition at Linate airport, in addition to the increased competition at
        Linate airport arising from the proposed slot transfer from the Parties to easyJet as
         outlined in Section 2. First, the Parties will continue to be the largest operator at
      LIN in terms of slot holding, with combined market shares over 50% both in IATA
      Summer and Winter seasons. Second, LIN is a level 3 coordinated airport under the
      IATA Worldwide Slot Guidelines, which  indicates  that demand  significantly
        exceeds the airport’s capacity and, therefore, that LIN is a congested airport, with
        very few slots available. Third, LIN is an especially well-located airport which is
         easily reachable from the city centre of Milan through a convenient public transport
        connection, therefore it serves Milan as its primary business airport and is a highly
          attractive airport for airlines and passengers alike. Accordingly, the transfer of the
         additional LIN slots, which will not create any prima facie competition concerns
         for the reasons further explained in paragraphs (97) and (116)(116), will add
        competitive pressure on the Parties’ operations at LIN.

(131)   In light of the above, the transfer of the additional LIN slots constitutes an integral
         part of the business case for IAG and AFKLM to become a Long-Haul Remedy
        Taker and to sign the Long-Haul Remedy Agreements. The LIN slot transfer is
         therefore related to the implementation of the Long-Haul Commitments which
       were adopted pursuant to the Merger Regulation.

3.4.    Conclusion on the Long-Haul Remedy Takers

(132)   In light of the above considerations, the Commission concludes that each of the
       Long-Haul Remedy-Takers fulfils the long-haul remedy-taker criteria and that the
        Long-haul Remedy Agreements are consistent with the Commitments including
          their objective to bring about a lasting structural change to the market.

4.     CONCLUSION

(133)  On the basis of the above assessment, (i) the Commission approves easyJet as a
         suitable Short-Haul Remedy Taker for the above-mentioned reasons and (ii) the
       Commission approves each of IAG and AFKLM as suitable Long-Haul Remedy
        Takers for the above-mentioned reasons.

(134)  On  the  basis  of  the Proposed  Short-Haul and Long-Haul Agreements,  the
       Commission further concludes that the remedy is being implemented in a manner
         consistent with the Commitments.
